# Retention Loop Audit

Use this when someone shares a game design, GDD, feature spec or live game and wants to know why players do or don't come back, or how to make a feature stickier.

## Step 1 - Map the loops
Draw (or list) the following:
- **Moment-to-moment loop** (seconds)
- **Session loop** (minutes): how a session starts and, importantly, how it *ends*
- **Return loop** (days): what brings players back
- **Long-term loop** (months): mastery, collection, social status, story

## Step 2 - Score each loop on SDT needs (PENS model)
For each loop, rate 0–3 and give evidence:
- **Autonomy:** meaningful choices, multiple valid strategies, no coercion.
- **Competence:** clear feedback, rising challenge, visible skill growth, so outcomes depend on the player and not only on luck.
- **Relatedness:** friends, guilds, shared challenges, spectating, sharing.
Research: satisfying these needs predicts enjoyment and future play. *Frustrating* them predicts problematic gaming. So weak needs plus strong compulsion levers means a spike followed by churn.

## Step 3 - Identify the compulsion levers
Tag each one present and rate its intensity:
- Variable-ratio rewards, rarity reveals, near-misses (real or fake), losses disguised as wins.
- Sunk cost (pity counters, investment), FOMO (limited banners, countdowns), streak punishment.
- Social proof (server-wide pull announcements), VIP tiers, confusing currency.
For each one: is it **paired with player agency** (healthy tension) or **substituting for it** (compulsion)?

## Step 4 - Diagnose
Use this grid:
- High needs + moderate levers → durable loyalty (target).
- Low needs + high levers → short-term spike, burnout, regulatory risk (common gacha pattern; note gacha fatigue and shutdown rates).
- High needs + low levers → great game with weak hooks. Add an honest variable-reward layer, escalation or a social layer.
- Low on both → rethink the core.

## Step 5 - Recommend
For each problem, give one concrete change. Prefer: agency over odds; natural session endings plus "bank it" moments; rested bonuses instead of streaks; seeded shared challenges; visible progress over hidden pity. Mark any recommendation with rating or legal impact, and point to `game-chance-compliance-check` if that skill is available.

## Step 6 - Metrics to instrument
D1/D7/D30 retention; session length distribution (watch for a long tail of very long sessions); share of sessions ending at a natural endpoint versus being abandoned; how much run outcomes vary because of player choice; spending concentration (share of revenue from the top 1% of spenders, a red flag if extreme); player sentiment ("fun" versus "can't stop" versus "feel bad").
